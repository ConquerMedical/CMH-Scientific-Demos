Method and provenance
  • Zoom and cut-away (pinch, the + and − buttons, or the mouse wheel after clicking the view; ◐ slices the tumor open) enlarge the current 3-D view to show how its compartments nest. They do not reveal detail finer than the MRI, whose resolution is on the millimetre scale.
  • Cells (✺ switches between cells and measurement dots) are illustrative renderings in the style of fluorescence microscopy, not imaged cells. Magenta cells are tumor cells: rounded in the tumor body, elongated where they infiltrate the surrounding edema, and shown dividing at a rate that rises when the enhancing tumor is growing. Grey, fragmenting cells are dying; they increase when the enhancing tumor shrinks or the necrotic core grows. Pale blue cells with branching processes are healthy brain cells around and among the infiltrating tumor cells. Infiltrating cells spread outward faster when edema is growing. The rates come from the displayed volumes (observed between scans, or modeled in the explorations); the line under the legend states them. Zooming in brings the cells forward and fades the outlines.
  • Starting state: measured LUMIERE Patient-067 compartments at week 156 (progressive disease). The observed post-operative scans at week 157 (1 week after resection) and week 167 (11 weeks after) are shown for comparison only; neither was used to set the model.
  • Observed geometry. By default the 3-D body is Patient-067's own week-156 tumor anatomy, reconstructed from LUMIERE's atlas-space DeepBraTumIA segmentation and simplified for display. The cut shows the chosen approach; the counterfactual resection removes tissue from that observed geometry and subsequent compartment changes scale the displayed anatomy. The Geometry control can switch to equivalent-volume geometry.
  • The ring is a demonstration risk map around the tumor with two high-risk sectors. It stands in for patient-specific eloquent-cortex and tract mapping, which this dataset does not provide. Its values are not clinical risk estimates.
  • Removal fraction = extent × depth, reduced where the trajectory crosses high-risk tissue (resection is modeled as stopping short there). Wider and deeper resections add reactive regrowth near the cavity and raise the corridor score; the score sets clear (<0.22), borderline (<0.36), or high-risk.
  • Observed geometry. The 3-D view shows Patient-067's own tumor compartments and brain surface at week 156, reconstructed from LUMIERE's atlas-space DeepBraTumIA segmentation (surfaces simplified for display; outlines are the surfaces' true silhouettes). The modeled resection removes tissue from these surfaces along the chosen trajectory, and modeled volume changes scale each compartment about its own centroid. Switch to the equivalent-volume model with the Geometry control.
  • Post-operative course. Residual enhancing tumor regrows at rates taken from this patient's own recorded responses to her first three operations (weeks 0, 19 and 59; enhancing growth from each post-operative scan to the next scan): the shaded band spans those responses and the line weights the most recent most. Her fourth operation (week 157) is held out and shown as a check. A declared ceiling applies; edema follows regrowth and rises with surgical injury along high-risk trajectories. These are declared demonstration response functions, not a fitted outcome model. Her observed recurrence by week 167 was faster than this rate predicts, which is consistent with the operation having changed the tumor's behavior, the kind of change ABP is designed to detect rather than assume away.
  • The search tries all extents, depths and directions in 5° steps and returns the largest removal that stays clear.
