What you're seeing

  • Bright dot at the center: cell body (soma). The neuron's core, where inputs are added up and a spike is triggered.
  • Thick line upward: trunk (apical dendrite). The main cable collecting input from far away.
  • Fine lines: dendrites and branches. Where the neuron receives signals from other cells.
  • Small glowing ends: growing tips. Branches extending during the growth time-lapse.
  • Grey thread: axon. Carries the spike to other neurons.
  • Brief orange flash and pulse: the spike (action potential). A millisecond electrical event. A microscope does not see this directly; it is drawn here so you know when it happened.
  • Green glow spreading out: calcium fluorescence. Calcium enters after the spike and GCaMP lights up. This slower glow is what the microscope actually records.
  • Faint drifting specks: decoration for depth only.

The glow behind it

  1. 1962Osamu Shimomura isolates green fluorescent protein (GFP) from the jellyfish Aequorea victoria.
  2. 2001Junichi Nakai and colleagues fuse a rearranged GFP to calmodulin and M13, creating GCaMP: it glows when calcium binds.
  3. 2008Nobel Prize in Chemistry to Shimomura, Martin Chalfie and Roger Tsien for the discovery and development of GFP.
  4. 2026OptiCeil asks the next question: once you can see the firing, how long do you actually need to look?
Method and provenance
  • Measured values (dots, stop points, retained fractions, residual ambiguity, accuracy drops) come from Ewing, "Information arrival sets the minimum acquisition time in calcium imaging," Research Square, 2026 (doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-10898326/v1): 914 CASCADE segments, 312 neurons, neuron-disjoint discovery and held-out validation, 30 Hz (one volume ≈ 33 ms).
  • Curves between measured points are shape-preserving interpolations to show direction. The recoverability index after its peak is drawn declining in proportion to the reported accuracy drop; the study reports that it declined but not by how much. Early accuracy for the two GCaMP6s regimes follows the index shape.
  • The neuron, spike trains and traces are illustrative, not CASCADE recordings. Indicator rise and decay times are approximate, chosen to show that GCaMP6s is slower than GCaMP8m. "Intense" is a dramatized burst pattern.
  • Growth is a compressed time-lapse. In the developing brain, calcium signalling triggered by activity helps regulate dendritic growth and branching over days, alongside activity-independent cues such as neurotrophins (e.g. Wong & Ghosh, Nat Rev Neurosci 2002). The OptiCeil results concern spike detection, not growth.
  • Flashing is limited to at most three flashes per second, and motion is reduced when the device requests it.
  • The spinal regime is shown with lower signal-to-noise for illustration. The study found that the spinal label itself did not explain lower recoverability; a matched residual difference remained and is described there as observational.
  • The recoverability index is comparative, not an absolute physical bound, hence CEILING_UNRESOLVED.
  • History: Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2008 (nobelprize.org); GCaMP: Nakai, Ohkura & Imoto, 2001.
